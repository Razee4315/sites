06:10The name

Deo, a giant. Sai, its shadow.

Land of giantsLand of giants

The Shina-speaking herders who come up from Astore named it for what it does to you. Three and a half thousand square kilometres of grass at the height of an Alpine summit, and not one tree to tell you how far away anything is.

A boulder turns out to be a hill. The hill turns out to be forty minutes away. You are the smallest thing you can see, and you stay that way for days.

On the Skardu side the Balti call it Byarsa, the summer’s place, which is the more practical name: from November to May it belongs to the snow. We borrowed the word for ourselves.

07:30The ground

For six weeks the plateau is mostly flowers.

The thaw reaches four thousand metres in late June. Everything that grows here has until the first September frost to flower, set seed and go back under, so it all happens at once. Walk slowly. Most of it is ankle height.

11:40The count

1970+

In 1993 there were nineteen brown bears left on the plateau. The park was drawn around them that year. The recent counts put the number above seventy.

They stay because people keep their distance. We watch from three hundred metres or more, never leave the track in a vehicle, and never keep food in the open. If a bear changes what it is doing because of us, we are too close.

13:10Water

Sheosar, the blind lake.

No inlet you can find and no outlet you can follow, which is one explanation for the name. On a windless morning it holds Nanga Parbat upside down, from sixty kilometres away.

17:30Crossings

Three ways across.

Small groups, four to eight. A jeep and driver from Skardu, canvas tents, a cook, a kettle that is never cold, and a guide who grew up underneath the plateau. Park fees and sleeping bags rated to minus ten are in the price.

    18:50Camp

    Four tents, one kettle, no signal.

    Camp is up before you arrive. Dinner is dal, rice and whatever came up from Skardu that week, eaten in every layer you own. The herders who summer here with their flocks sometimes walk over to compare weather.

    22:40Night

    The nearest streetlight is two thousand metres below you.

    When the kettle is empty there is nothing left to do but look up. No moon for half the month, no haze, hardly any air. The Milky Way is bright enough to see your hands by. People stop talking.
