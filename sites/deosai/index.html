    Ghbiarsa · the summer’s place

    Deosai is a treeless plateau about four thousand metres up, where the western Himalaya runs into the Karakoram. The Balti name for it, Ghbiarsa, means the summer’s place, because summer is the only time anyone can get there.

    It is often described as the world’s second-highest plateau, after Tibet’s Changtang. Nobody lives on it. Herders bring their animals up for the warm months, bears walk in from the valleys in June, and by October the road is shut again.

    • 4,000mRoughly how high the plains lie. One much-quoted average is 4,114 m.
    • 1993The year it became a national park, to protect the last of its brown bears.
    • 8monthsUnder snow, in the usual telling. Sources put the white season anywhere from October to May.
    • 0Permanent human residents on the plains.

    How it got its name

    One giant, not many.

    In Shina the name reads as deo, giant, and sai, shadow. The Gujjar herders tell it as Dev Vasai, the land the giant settled: a simple-minded giant took the flat grassland for a farm and began ploughing at the thaw. A fox warned him that summer here lasts barely eight weeks. The snow came before the harvest, and he fled.

    What is real,
    what is paper

    Every photograph on this site is a real photograph, used under an open licence and credited below. Everything that looks like cut paper was generated for this site.

    The paper animals, plants, landscapes and the four-light lake scene are illustrations made with an AI image model (OpenAI, through Codex) from written descriptions of each species, then checked against photographs. They are not photographs and are never labelled as one. Most species photographs were taken elsewhere in the animal’s or plant’s range, not in Deosai; the photographs of places are of Deosai itself. The three fish are shown as nineteenth-century scientific plates, because no openly licensed photograph of them exists.
