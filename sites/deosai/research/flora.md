# Flora of the Deosai Plateau / Deosai National Park: verified research notes

Compiled 10 October 2026 from web sources only. Purpose: feed a public website where every statement must be true.

## How to read this file

**Inclusion rule.** A species is in the main lists (Parts 2 to 4) only if at least one of these places it on the plateau itself:

- a peer-reviewed paper that sampled or collected on the plateau (mainly Shaheen et al. 2019, Nawaz et al. 2019, Bano et al. 2012 and 2015, Farooq 1993, the lichen papers);
- a herbarium specimen whose label names a plateau locality (Deosai Plains, Deosai Plateau, Bara Pani, Kala/Chala Pani, Sheosar Lake, Ali Malik, Deosai Top), read from the GBIF occurrence record;
- a dated, captioned field photograph from the University of British Columbia (UBC) Karakoram survey of July 2016.

Species known only from buffer-zone villages or approach valleys are in Part 5. Doubtful or unverifiable names are in Part 6.

**Evidence labels used in each block**

- `[paper]` peer-reviewed publication.
- `[specimen]` herbarium record on GBIF; the link opens the individual record.
- `[photo]` UBC field photograph with a Deosai caption.
- `[general]` a description of the species in general (colour, height, flowering months) taken from a reference site. It is NOT a Deosai observation. Use it as background only.

**Checks still worth doing before publication**

1. Shaheen et al. 2019, Table 1: the PDF text layer scrambles the columns for species 66 to 132. I re-paired names with family, altitude and importance value by row order, and the pairing passed every anchor check except one (see Part 7, Taraxacum). Altitudes and importance values quoted for those species are marked "(table row re-paired)". Confirm against the PDF before printing a number.
2. UBC blog captions reached me through a summarising fetch tool. The facts (place, altitude, date) were consistent across repeat fetches, but re-check the exact wording before printing any caption as a quotation.
3. Dates on herbarium records are collection dates. A plant collected in late July was usually in flower or fruit, but the record does not say which. I write "collected in" and never "flowers in" for these.
4. Local names are given exactly as spelled in the cited paper. Different papers spell and assign them differently (Part 7).

**Abbreviations.** IVI = importance value index in Shaheen et al. 2019 (a measure of how dominant a species was in their quadrats; the highest was 18.28). GBIF = Global Biodiversity Information Facility. HO = Tasmanian Herbarium. SZU = University of Salzburg herbarium. QAU = Quaid-i-Azam University herbarium, Islamabad. NY = New York Botanical Garden. US = Smithsonian. K = Kew. S = Swedish Museum of Natural History. MO = Missouri Botanical Garden.

## Part 1. General vegetation facts

### 1.1 How many plant species?

Herbarium evidence (my own tally, not a published figure): GBIF holds 3,295 plant occurrence records with "Deosai" in the locality field, 2,781 of them preserved specimens, collected between 1838 and 2012 ([GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)). Many labels use "Deosai" for a wider region that includes Burzil Pass, the Shingo valley and Chilam. After I removed those, 1,944 records carrying about 517 species names remained for plateau localities. Do not publish "517 species" as a fact: the names are not all current and the filter is mine.

### 1.2 Dominant families and life forms

- In the 2016 quadrat survey Asteraceae led with 17 species, then Polygonaceae 12, Ranunculaceae 8, Rosaceae and Scrophulariaceae 7 each. These five families made up 38% of the flora recorded ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
- Life forms in the same survey: hemicryptophytes (perennials whose buds sit at ground level) 54%, geophytes (bulbs, rhizomes) 19%, therophytes (annuals) 15%, chamaephytes (cushions and dwarf shrubs) 9%. Small-leaved plants (leptophylls and nanophylls) made up 72% of species ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
- The single most dominant plant in that survey was the grass *Poa alpina* (IVI 18.28), followed by *Phleum alpinum* (8.91), *Taraxacum tibetanum* (7.74), *Kobresia schoenoides* (7.44), *Draba oreades* (7.16) and *Nepeta connata* (7.13) ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
- "Herbaceous perennial grasses and sedges dominate the vegetation of the plateau" ([Usman et al. 2024, MycoKeys](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10924169/)).
- Eight plant families were commonly eaten by brown bears: Poaceae, Polygonaceae, Cyperaceae, Apiaceae, Asteraceae, Caryophyllaceae, Lamiaceae and Rubiaceae, with grasses and sedges the bulk of the diet ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
- Endemism in the 2016 survey: 41 species (33%) endemic to the Himalaya as a whole, 18 (14%) to the Western Himalaya, 27 (22%) tropical Asian, 34 (27%) cosmopolitan. Note the abstract says 18 Western Himalayan endemics and the results text says eight; see Part 7 ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
- The UNESCO tentative-list entry says the flora is influenced by four floristic elements: Boreoalpine/Circumpolar, Euro-Siberian, Southern European/Mediterranean and Siberian-Mongolian ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6115/)).

### 1.3 Vegetation zones and communities

- **Four habitat types used in brown bear research:** marshy (dominated by *Poa* and *Carex*, the most productive), grassy (dominated by grasses, second), stony (a wide variety of herbaceous flowering plants, third) and rocky (generally without vegetation) ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)). Only about 65% of the park carried productive vegetation ([Nawaz et al. 2014, abstract](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dfe5ed63b8185d9cafe35b)).
- **Eight plant communities named in 2016**, each after its three dominant genera: Poa-Draba-Rhodiola (3,987 m), Waldheimia-Artemisia-Polygonum (3,907 m), Salix-Carex-Kobresia (3,880 m), Kobresia-Primula-Primula (4,153 m), Allium-Phleum-Carex (4,170 m), Poa-Anaphalis-Leontopodium (3,790 m), Caltha-Carex-Pedicularis (3,775 m) and Taraxacum-Nepeta-Poa (3,781 m). Moisture and altitude were the main factors separating them; the moist sites were the richest ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
- **UNESCO description:** large expanses of alpine meadow alternate with drier stony areas, with patches of dwarf willows along rivers and streams and many species of *Poa*, *Saxifraga* and *Euphorbia* ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6115/)).
- **Collectors' habitat notes on specimen labels:** "Carex-Aconitum sedgeland" on wet peat at the east shore of Sheosar Lake; "sedge-grassland with dwarf Salix, Swertia and Carex" above the north shore; "wet sedgeland" by the river below Bara Pani camp (Pakistan-New Zealand wetlands expedition, September 1998; [example record](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2430753358)). G. and S. Miehe's 1990 and 1991 labels describe "forb-rich steppe (meadow steppe)" with *Kobresia*, "relatively humid alpine Cyperaceae mats dominated by Carex and Kobresia", and "open cushion and rosette plant formations in the free gelifluction belt" on the eastern plains (GBIF moss records, Part 4).
- Valley bogs and ponds support sedges, grasses and herbs; the ground is a thermokarst landscape of wetlands and small lakes fed by snow and ice melt and permafrost ([Fida et al. 2025, Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2024.1477480/full)).
- Wikipedia places the plateau in the Karakoram-West Tibetan Plateau alpine steppe ecoregion (cross-check only; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deosai_National_Park)).

### 1.4 When the bloom happens and how long the growing season is

- Growing season June to September; plants are dwarfed and most species bloom at the same time ([Khan et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/)).
- The park is snow-covered for most of the year, "generally October-May"; brown bears forage there from June to early October ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
- Winter lasts more than half the year, September to May; summer runs from mid-May to September ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)). Snow-covered and inaccessible September to May ([Bano 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4039317/)).
- Herbarium collecting dates agree with a midsummer peak. Of the 2,168 dated GBIF plant records with "Deosai" in the locality, 1,030 were collected in July, 859 in August, 248 in September and 20 in June (my tally of the [GBIF records](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).
- UNESCO and the Gilgit-Baltistan Forest Department both say "in the Spring season it is covered by sweeps of wildflowers" ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6115/), [GB Forest Dept](https://fwegb.gov.pk/deosai-national-park/)). In calendar terms the scientific sources put this in June to August. Safest wording for the site: "the bloom comes in the short summer, mainly July and August, after the snow melts".
- Climate: mean daily temperature from about -20 °C in winter to 9 to 12 °C in summer. Annual precipitation is given as 510 to 750 mm ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf), [Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)) or 350 to 550 mm, mostly as winter snow ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6115/), [Usman 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10924169/)).

### 1.5 Why there are no trees

- The plateau is "well above the tree line" ([Bano et al. 2015](https://thejaps.org.pk/manuscript/2015-japs-60.pdf)) and the park lies "above the timberline" ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
- The vegetation is alpine, "resembling arctic tundra", and treeless except for a small corner toward Dras and Kargil ([Khan et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/)).
- Shrubs are restricted to narrow belts along streams. Fruit-bearing plants are not available in the park. Coniferous forest occurs only in the surrounding valleys ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
- The sources state the fact (above the treeline) and describe the conditions: more than half the year under snow, a growing season of roughly four months, mean winter temperatures near -20 °C. Shaheen et al. describe alpine plants in general as stunted, woolly or spiny and dwarfed as an adaptation to cold, wind, high ultraviolet radiation and a short season. None of the sources I read tests which factor sets the treeline at Deosai, so keep the wording to "too high, too cold and too briefly snow-free for trees".
- Tree species do appear in two ethnobotanical lists (*Betula utilis*, *Juniperus excelsa*), but those surveys included villages and approach valleys below the plateau. See Part 5.

### 1.6 Grazing and nomadic herders

- Gujjar-Bakarwal herders "travel large distances to utilize the Deosai National Park as grazing lands" ([Wikipedia, citing Earth Journalism Network 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deosai_National_Park)).
- The Himalayan Wildlife Foundation zoned the park into areas for wildlife and "grazing areas for communities and nomadic herdsmen - Gujjars (seasonal nomadic herdsmen)", and lists "Gujjar Control and Management" among its activities ([HWF](https://hwf.org.pk/?p=2025)).
- Dawn (2015): communities on the north-eastern edge traditionally graze livestock and are confined to certain areas, while Gujjar-Bakarwal herders bring animals in summer and graze "in almost all the areas of Deosai"; HWF staff proposed grazing permits ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1164658)). This is journalism quoting HWF, not a measured study.
- UNESCO: there is no permanent settlement on the plains, and traditional grazing rights inside the park are restricted to seven communities using designated sectors as summer pasture ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6115/)).
- Shaheen et al.: "nearly fifty different neighboring communities" have traditional grazing rights along with nomadic herdsmen in summer (citing Khan et al. 2010 and Akhlas 2009). This conflicts with UNESCO's seven; see Part 7.
- Effects on plants: grazing pressure was the main threat to palatable species, yet sites with moderate grazing had higher species richness than expected ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)). Brown bears strongly avoid heavily grazed areas ([Nawaz 2014 abstract](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65dfe5ed63b8185d9cafe35b)), and about 82% of livestock grazing areas fall inside bear habitat patches ([Fida et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2024.1477480/full)).
- None of the peer-reviewed papers I read gives a headcount of herders or livestock on the plateau.

### 1.7 Threatened plants

- Four species recorded in 2016 are on the IUCN Red List as threatened and all had importance values below 1%: *Saussurea costus* (as *S. lappa*), *Aconitum chasmanthum*, *Aconitum heterophyllum* and *Lilium polyphyllum*. Sixty-three species in total had importance values below 1% ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)). See Part 6 for caveats on *Saussurea costus* and *Lilium polyphyllum*.
- Stated threats: overgrazing, collection of medicinal plants (often by uprooting whole plants), invasive species, habitat degradation and climate change ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf), [Khan 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/)).

## Part 2. Plants: grasses, sedges, rushes, shrubs, cushion plants, ferns and medicinal herbs

### Grasses (Poaceae)

- **Alpine meadow-grass** — *Poa alpina* L. — Poaceae — grass
  - The single most dominant plant in the 2016 quadrat survey (average IVI 18.28), present in all eight communities from 3,770 to 4,170 m `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - Collected on a river bank at Barwai (Chogochu) Lungma, 3,900 m, on gravel terraces and wet silt at the edge of a flood channel, 7 Sept 1998 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2430874566)); also by R.R. Stewart on the Deosai Plains, 28 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2900463652)).
  - Detected in brown bear droppings by DNA ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).

- **Alpine timothy / Alpine cat's-tail** — *Phleum alpinum* L. — Poaceae — grass
  - Second most dominant species in 2016 (IVI 8.91, table row re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - Photographed at the north-west edge of the plateau at 4,300 m, July 2016 `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/12/phleum_alpinum/)).
  - Collected on wet peat with seasonal pools at the east shore of Sheosar Lake, 4,050 m, 6 Sept 1998 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4865089027)); a 1936 label reads "Meadow. Deosai Plain" ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2900452510)) `[specimen]`.

- **Kashmir fescue** (no established English name) — *Festuca kashmiriana* Stapf — Poaceae — grass
  - Nineteen specimen records from the plateau, the earliest collected by J.F. Duthie on the shores of Sheosar ("Shersán") lake on 15 Sept 1893 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4608046453)).
  - Later collections at 3,900 m (U. Schickhoff, 27 Aug 1993; [GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/6561696295)) and 3,850 m (J.P. Gruber, 17 July 1999; [GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990623838)).

- **Long-awned wild rye** (no established English name) — *Elymus longearistatus* (Boiss.) Tzvelev (spelled *Elymus longi-aristatus* in the bear paper; listed as *Agropyron longearistatum* by the GB Forest Department) — Poaceae — grass
  - Found in 23% of brown bear dropping samples `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Collected on "dry ground", Deosai Plain, 3,658 m, 9 Aug 1936 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/3969819153)), and by C.B. Clarke on 31 July 1876 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4853895428)) `[specimen]`.
  - On the Gilgit-Baltistan Forest Department's plant list for the park ([GB Forest Dept](https://fwegb.gov.pk/deosai-national-park/)).

- **Supine meadow-grass** — *Poa supina* Schrad. — Poaceae — grass
  - Found in 47% of brown bear dropping samples, the most frequent grass identified to species `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Collected by R.R. Stewart on the Deosai Plains at about 3,960 m in July 1940 `[specimen]` (NY and US sheets, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).

- **Brown bent** — *Agrostis vinealis* Schreb. — Poaceae — grass
  - Found in 31% of brown bear dropping samples `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)). Identification is by a short DNA marker matched to a reference collection of 112 plant specimens gathered in the park, so treat the species-level name with some caution.
  - Unnamed *Agrostis* was collected in grassland "with dwarf Salix, Gentiana and Carex" above the north shore of Sheosar Lake at 4,150 m in Sept 1998 `[specimen]` (HO, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).

- **June grass** — *Koeleria macrantha* (Ledeb.) Schult. — Poaceae — grass
  - Found in 5% of brown bear dropping samples `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Kew holds specimens from the plateau collected in August 1990 at 3,800 m `[specimen]`. Closely related material is filed on GBIF as *Koeleria pyramidata* (six records, 3,780 to 4,050 m; e.g. [GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990623997)).

### Sedges (Cyperaceae)

- **Bog sedge** (formerly a *Kobresia*) — *Carex deasyi* (C.B.Clarke) O.Yano & S.R.Zhang on GBIF; called *Kobresia schoenoides* in every Deosai source — Cyperaceae — sedge
  - Fourth most dominant species in 2016 (IVI 7.44, 3,880 to 4,170 m, table row re-paired) and one of the name-giving plants of the Salix-Carex-Kobresia and Kobresia-Primula-Primula communities `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - Twenty-two specimen records, originally labelled *Kobresia schoenoides*, including one from the "open plateau surface" north of the radar station at 4,005 m, 16 July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990623887)), and one from 1936 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1675912320)) `[specimen]`.
  - G. and S. Miehe's 1990 label describes a humid meadow "rich in herbs and Graminae, with Kobresia schoenoides" `[specimen]` (Part 4, *Polytrichum juniperinum*).
  - Name note: the mapping of *Kobresia schoenoides* to *Carex deasyi* is GBIF's. Confirm the accepted name in Plants of the World Online before using it; "*Kobresia schoenoides*" is the safe label for the website.

- **Black-flowered sedge** (no established English name) — *Carex melanantha* C.A.Mey. — Cyperaceae — sedge
  - Recorded from 3,770 to 4,170 m with IVI 4.08 `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - Nineteen specimen records spanning 1876 to 1999, e.g. the Kala (Chala) Pani river valley at 3,800 m, 17 July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990638704)), and Stewart's 1940 sheet ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1675969140)) `[specimen]`.

- **A sedge** — *Carex diluta* M.Bieb. — Cyperaceae — sedge
  - The most frequently identified plant species in brown bear droppings (63% of samples), and eaten significantly more by male bears than females `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Marshy vegetation, dominated by *Poa* and *Carex*, produces the most forage in the park and is the bears' preferred habitat ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - DNA-based identification; I found no herbarium specimen under this name from the plateau.

- **Round sedge** (no established English name) — *Carex orbicularis* Boott — Cyperaceae — sedge
  - Sixteen specimen records, including one from the plateau at 4,000 m on 21 July 1998 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1096831225)) and one from the Kala (Chala) Pani river valley at 3,800 m in 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990623596)) `[specimen]`.

- **Pamir sedge** (no established English name) — *Carex pamirensis* C.B.Clarke ex B.Fedtsch. — Cyperaceae — sedge
  - R.R. Stewart's label reads "In swamps", Deosai Plains `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/3820295341)).
  - Sixteen specimen records from 1876 to 1999, 3,658 to 4,110 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990624008)).

- **Bristle sedge** — *Carex microglochin* Wahlenb. — Cyperaceae — sedge (small)
  - Collected by Stewart on the Deosai Plains at about 3,960 m, 29 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1676014474)), and near Kala (Chala) Pani camp at 3,830 m in July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615290316)) `[specimen]`.

- **White cottongrass** — *Eriophorum scheuchzeri* Hoppe — Cyperaceae — sedge
  - One Smithsonian specimen: Deosai Plain, 3,658 m, 9 Aug 1936, collector W.N. Koelz `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1675796056)).
  - Single old record. Safe to say it has been collected there; not safe to say it is common.

### Rushes (Juncaceae)

- **Deosai rush** — *Juncus deosaicus* Noltie (now also combined as *Alpinojuncus deosaicus*) — Juncaceae — rush
  - Named after Deosai. Described by Henry Noltie in the Edinburgh Journal of Botany 55(1): 41 (1998) ([IPNI](https://www.ipni.org/n/999929-1)).
  - Type collected on the Deosai Plains, 0 to 9 km beyond the top of the Satpara valley, 35°06'N 75°33'E, 3,900 m, on 19 Aug 1994 by Hussain et al.; holotype at the Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh ([IPNI](https://www.ipni.org/n/999929-1)).

- **A rush** — *Juncus membranaceus* Royle ex D.Don — Juncaceae — rush
  - Listed in the vegetation cover of the plateau in 1993 `[paper]` ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf)).
  - QAU label, Deosai top, 8 Aug 1977: "Herb, erect, heads brown, common" ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2234221724)); also the Kala (Chala) Pani valley at 3,800 m in 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990623874)) `[specimen]`.

- **Spiked woodrush** — *Luzula spicata* (L.) DC. — Juncaceae — rush relative
  - Nine specimen records, e.g. 5 km north-east of "Deosai lake" at 3,925 m, 17 July 1999 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990623697)).
  - Unnamed *Luzula* was also collected on peat soil over old moraines at Bari La, 4,800 m, in Sept 1998 (HO, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).

### Dwarf shrubs

- **Dwarf willows** — *Salix flabellaris* Andersson, *Salix caesia* Vill., *Salix hastata* L., *Salix karelinii* Turcz. ex Stschegl. — Salicaceae — dwarf shrubs
  - *S. flabellaris*: recorded from 3,770 to 4,170 m with IVI 5.71 (table row re-paired) and it names the Salix-Carex-Kobresia community `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)); Stewart collected it between Yusi Mar and Satpara Pass at about 4,115 m on 1 Aug 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/3898432891)).
  - *S. caesia*: "low wet ground near stream", Bara Deosai, 20 Aug 1955 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/5284365090)); Kala (Chala) Pani valley at 3,800 m, 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990638963)) `[specimen]`.
  - *S. hastata*: QAU label, Deosai top, 3 Aug 1978: "Shrub, fruit in catkin, common", on stony soil ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1978568919)). *S. karelinii*: eastern Deosai, 4,200 m, 15 July 1991 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615392221)).
  - UNESCO describes "patches of dwarf willows along the rivers and streams" ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6115/)). Shrubs are poorly represented in bear diet ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).

- **Juniper** — *Juniperus* sp.; sources disagree on the species (see Part 7) — Cupressaceae — dwarf shrub
  - *Juniperus squamata* was recorded at 4,170 m with a very low IVI of 0.21 (table row re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - The park's baseline report documents three junipers, *J. communis*, *J. excelsa* and *J. turkestanica*, as quoted in [Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/).
  - Kew holds a G. Miehe specimen labelled *Juniperus pseudosabina*, "Plains of Deosai", 3,700 m, 13 Aug 1990 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/5843825655)).
  - Safe wording: "juniper occurs sparsely as a low shrub". Do not name one species without a caveat.

- **Gerard's joint-pine / Ephedra** — *Ephedra gerardiana* Wall. ex Stapf — Ephedraceae — dwarf shrub (a gymnosperm, not a flowering plant)
  - Local names: **Chay** (Balti), **Soom** (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).
  - Recorded from 3,770 to 3,990 m with IVI 3.25 `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)). Unnamed *Ephedra* was collected opposite Bara Pani camp at 3,900 m on a "dry stoney surface of interfluve" in Sept 1998 (HO, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).
  - One of only three possible fruit sources found in brown bear diet, in under 3% of samples ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Reported uses: fruit and root as a tonic and for eye pain (Balti); for asthma and bronchitis (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)). Third-highest use value of 51 medicinal plants in [Khan 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/).

- **Dwarf honeysuckles** — *Lonicera semenovii* Regel, *Lonicera microphylla* Willd. ex Schult., *Lonicera asperifolia* (Decne.) Hook.f. & Thomson — Caprifoliaceae — dwarf shrubs
  - *L. semenovii* collected around "Deosai Top Hotel", east of Ali Malik, at 3,910 to 3,930 m on 16 July 1999 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615354228)).
  - QAU labels, 4 Aug 1978: *L. microphylla*, Deosai top, "Shrub, in fruit, red flowers, common", rocky soil; *L. asperifolia*, "Deosai Vally", "Shrub, white flower, fruit, common", stony soil `[specimen]` (QAU, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).
  - Shaheen et al. list *Lonicera caerulea* at 4,170 m ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).

- **Cotoneaster** — *Cotoneaster integerrimus* Medik. and *Cotoneaster humilis* Dunn as named in sources — Rosaceae — dwarf shrub
  - *C. integerrimus* collected on a summit south-east of Kala (Chala) Pani at 3,945 m, 18 July 1999 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990624010)).
  - *C. humilis* listed at 3,780 m `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)). Unnamed *Cotoneaster* collected on a "dry rocky ridge" at 4,200 m south-east of Bara Pani camp in Sept 1998 (HO).
  - A cotoneaster turned up in 2% of brown bear dropping samples ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).

- **Dwarf rhododendron** — *Rhododendron anthopogon* D.Don subsp. *hypenanthum* (Balf.f.) Cullen (written "*Rododendron hypenanthum*" in the source) — Ericaceae — dwarf shrub
  - Recorded at 4,170 m with IVI 3.53 (table row re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - Supporting record with no collector or date in the Himalayan Uplands Plant database, locality "Deosai P." ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615388571)).
  - Local name **Chauman**, leaves and flowers used for cough and lung disorders, in a Balti-language survey of the Skardu valley that included four Deosai sites (the table does not say where each plant was found) ([Bano 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4039317/)).
  - One paper plus one undated database record. Use with the caveat "recorded at about 4,170 m in a 2016 survey".

### Cushion, mat and succulent plants

- **Prickly thrift** (no established English name) — *Acantholimon lycopodioides* (Girard) Boiss. — Plumbaginaceae — spiny cushion plant
  - Recorded only at the highest and driest sampling sites, 4,150 to 4,170 m (IVI 0.84), and classed as a chamaephyte, the cushion and dwarf-shrub life form `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - Shaheen et al. describe alpine plants here in general as "stunted, wooly or spiny, dwarfed" as an adaptation to cold, wind and strong ultraviolet light.

- **Wedge-leaved sibbaldia and creeping sibbaldia** — *Sibbaldia cuneata* Hornem. ex Kuntze and *Sibbaldia procumbens* L. — Rosaceae — mat-forming herbs
  - *S. cuneata*: in the 1993 list of the plateau's vegetation cover ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf)); recorded from 3,770 to 4,170 m in 2016 (table row re-paired; [Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)) `[paper]`; five specimen records at 3,850 to 4,050 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615402275)).
  - *S. procumbens*: photographed on the plateau at about 4,000 m on 22 July 2016 and described as common across the 2016 survey sites `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2021/07/23/sibbaldia-procumbens-l/)).

- **Roseroots** — *Rhodiola* spp., including *Rhodiola heterodonta* (Hook.f. & Thomson) Boriss., *Rhodiola imbricata* Edgew. and *Rhodiola wallichiana* (Hook.) S.H.Fu — Crassulaceae — succulent herbs
  - *Rhodiola* is one of the three name-giving genera of the Poa-Draba-Rhodiola community at 3,987 m `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - *R. imbricata* (labelled *R. recticaulis*) collected on the plateau at 4,000 m, 21 July 1998 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1096790519)) `[specimen]`.
  - *R. wallichiana* photographed at about 4,300 m on 28 July 2016 `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/13/unknown-spp-96/)); Stewart collected it at Deosai on 28 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2446824066)).

- **Pink stonecrop** — *Hylotelephium ewersii* (Ledeb.) H.Ohba (syn. *Sedum ewersii* Ledeb.) — Crassulaceae — succulent herb; small pink flowers
  - Photographed on the plateau at 4,200 m, July 2016; the blog notes its "distinctive fleshy leaves and small pink flowers" and that it is quite common on rocky slopes in the region `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/08/22/skinmang-4300-b/)).
  - Recorded at 3,770 m in 2016 ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)) and collected on the eastern plains at 4,100 m in July 1991 (GBIF) `[paper]` `[specimen]`.

### Ferns and clubmosses

- **Brittle bladder-fern** — *Cystopteris fragilis* (L.) Bernh. — Cystopteridaceae — fern
  - Collected from a "crevice in rocks of bluff" about 1 km upstream of Bara Pani camp, 10 Sept 1998 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2430945164)), and from a damp area on a dry rocky slope at 4,100 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2431075915)) `[specimen]`.
  - Also from a summit at 3,945 m south-east of Kala (Chala) Pani, 18 July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990625520)).

- **Fir clubmoss** — *Huperzia selago* (L.) Bernh. ex Schrank & Mart. — Lycopodiaceae — clubmoss
  - One specimen: Bari La pass, 4,800 m, "under boulders", 9 Sept 1998 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2430948505)). This is the highest-altitude plant record in this file.
  - A clubmoss family signal (Lycopodiaceae) also appeared in 2% of brown bear dropping samples ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).

### Medicinal and aromatic herbs

- **Himalayan thyme** — *Thymus linearis* Benth. — Lamiaceae — aromatic mat-forming herb; pink-purple flowers
  - Local names: **Tumburuk** (Balti), **Tumuro** (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).
  - Recorded from 3,770 to 4,170 m with IVI 5.55 (table row re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)). Photographed in a rocky, dry, frost-heaved area of the plateau interior at about 4,000 m on 22 July 2016 `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2021/07/18/thymus-linearis/)).
  - QAU labels from Deosai top, Aug 1978, filed as *Thymus serpyllum*: "Prostrate, herb, purple flowers, common" `[specimen]` (QAU, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)). The UBC author confirms the plateau plant as *T. linearis*.
  - Reported uses: whole plant for flatulence and abdominal pain (Balti); cough and asthma (Shina); one of the five most-used species in the survey ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).
  - `[general]` Pink-purple whorls of flowers, a shrublet about 15 to 30 cm, flowering April to September, 1,500 to 4,300 m ([Flowers of India](https://flowersofindia.net/catalog/slides/Himalayan%20Thyme.html)).

- **White hogweed** — *Tetrataenium candicans* (Wall. ex DC.) Manden. on GBIF (syn. *Heracleum candicans* Wall. ex DC., the name used in all Deosai sources) — Apiaceae — tall herb
  - Local name: **Ghang** (Balti) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).
  - Found in 50% of brown bear dropping samples, one of only three plants in more than half of them `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Recorded from 3,770 to 4,170 m (IVI 1.13) ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)); collected on the plateau at 4,050 m, 13 Aug 1990 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615331615)); photographed in a cattle-grazed area near the road at about 4,200 m in July 2016, with the identification flagged as uncertain ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/12/heracleum_candicans/)).
  - Reported use: root for boils, pimples and pustules (Balti) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).

- **Atis / Atees** — *Aconitum heterophyllum* Wall. ex Royle — Ranunculaceae — poisonous medicinal herb
  - Local names: **Patris** (Shina) ([Khan 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/)); "Patris or Baris" ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf), language not stated).
  - Recorded at 4,150 m (IVI 1.46) and named as one of four IUCN-threatened plants on the plateau `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - A 1926 label reads "Alpine meadows", Deosai plains, 13,000 ft ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/3327542475)); also 3,850 m near Kala (Chala) Pani, 17 July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990638590)) `[specimen]`. Photographed at about 4,000 m on 22 July 2016 ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/12/aconitum-heterophyllum/)).
  - Reported use: root for fever, indigestion and dysentery ([Khan 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/)).

- **Wild onions** — *Allium carolinianum* DC. and *Allium atrosanguineum* Schrenk (syn. *Allium fedtschenkoanum* Regel, the name used in Deosai papers) — Amaryllidaceae — bulbous herbs
  - Local names for *A. carolinianum*: **Refor** (Balti), **Kachpauk** (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)); **Phaloon** (Shina) for both species ([Khan 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/)).
  - *A. fedtschenkoanum* recorded from 3,880 to 4,170 m (IVI 3.89) and names the Allium-Phleum-Carex community at 4,170 m `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)); collected near Kala (Chala) Pani at 3,800 to 3,850 m in July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615262423)).
  - *A. carolinianum*: specimen records from "Deosai" at 3,810 m and 4,270 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615262005)) `[specimen]`.
  - Reported uses: bulb for pharyngitis and bronchitis (Balti), leaves for constipation (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).

- **Rhubarbs** — *Rheum tibeticum* Maxim. ex Hook.f. and *Rheum australe* D.Don (syn. *R. emodi* Wall.) — Polygonaceae — large-leaved herbs
  - Local names for *R. australe*: **Lachu** (Balti), **Chontal** (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).
  - Both recorded from 3,790 to 4,170 m (table rows re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - *R. tibeticum* collected on the Deosai Plains at 3,700 m, 12 July 1991 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615386755)), and on the Chilam to Skardu track on 31 Aug 1995 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/5060675978)) `[specimen]`.
  - Reported uses of *R. australe*: root for stomach ulcer (Balti); root and leaves as a laxative and for dyspepsia (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).

- **Nepal dock** — *Rumex nepalensis* Spreng. — Polygonaceae — herb
  - Local name: **Hubable** (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).
  - Recorded from 3,780 to 4,170 m (IVI 3.45, table row re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)); found in 18% of brown bear dropping samples ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Reported use: root for backache and abdominal pain (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).

- **Wormwoods / sagebrush** — *Artemisia* spp., including *Artemisia maritima* L. as named on specimens — Asteraceae — aromatic herbs and subshrubs
  - *Artemisia* is one of the three name-giving genera of the Waldheimia-Artemisia-Polygonum community at 3,907 m, a dry site `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - *A. maritima* collected by Stewart on the Deosai Plains, 30 July 1940 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4924694234)); it is also on the GB Forest Department list ([GB Forest Dept](https://fwegb.gov.pk/deosai-national-park/)).
  - QAU label, 4 Aug 1978, *Artemisia laciniata*: "herb, erect, flower yellow, common", stony soil (QAU, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).
  - The well-known Balti medicinal wormwood **Bursay** (*Artemisia brevifolia*) is recorded from the Satpara side and the upper Shingo valley, not from the plateau itself; see Part 5.

- **Dhoop / Jurinea** — *Dolomiaea macrocephala* DC. ex Royle (syn. *Jurinea dolomiaea* Boiss., *Jurinea macrocephala*) — Asteraceae — stemless rosette herb
  - Local names: **Sathing** (Balti), **Gogal Dhoop** (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)); "Dhup or Gugal" ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf)).
  - One of twelve daisy-family species collected on the plateau in 2012 and 2013 for a leaf-anatomy study `[paper]` ([Bano 2015](https://thejaps.org.pk/manuscript/2015-japs-60.pdf)).
  - Collected around Deosai Top Hotel at 3,910 to 3,930 m, 16 July 1999 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615310342)).
  - Reported uses: root for fever and cold (Balti); leaves for wounds and skin eruptions (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).

- **Northern bedstraw** — *Galium boreale* L. — Rubiaceae — herb; small white flowers
  - Local name: **Shatong** (Balti) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).
  - In the 1993 list of the plateau's vegetation cover ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf)); recorded at 3,770 to 3,780 m ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)); found in 10% of brown bear dropping samples ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)) `[paper]`.
  - Collected on a summit at 3,945 m south-east of Kala (Chala) Pani, 18 July 1999 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990611742)).
  - Reported use: fruits for hepatitis (Balti) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).

- **Rumex-leaved knotweed** — *Koenigia rumicifolia* (Royle ex Bab.) T.M.Schust. & Reveal on GBIF (syn. *Aconogonon rumicifolium*, *Polygonum rumicifolium*) — Polygonaceae — tall leafy herb
  - Found in 23% of brown bear dropping samples `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Twelve specimen records at 3,700 to 4,300 m, e.g. the Skardu side of the plateau at 4,000 m, 21 July 1998 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1096790532)).
  - The UBC survey photographed a related tall knotweed, labelled *Aconogonon alpinum*, on the plateau on 22 July 2016 and noted it tended to grow in dense single-species stands on arid south-facing slopes ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2021/07/23/aconogonon-alpinum-all-schur/)). Which of the two names fits the plateau plant is not settled by these sources.

## Part 3. Flowers: the showy and the small

### Showy wildflowers

- **Himalayan fleeceflower / Himalayan bistort** — *Bistorta affinis* (D.Don) Greene (syn. *Polygonum affine* D.Don, *Persicaria affinis*) — Polygonaceae — mat-forming herb; pink flower spikes
  - Local names: **Chomoi** (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/), [Khan 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/)); **Strin mindoq** in a Balti-language survey ([Bano 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4039317/)).
  - Found in 47% of brown bear dropping samples and eaten significantly more often by male bears `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)). Recorded from 3,770 to 4,150 m ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - Photographed on a mesic mid-slope at the north-east edge at 4,300 m, July 2016 `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/13/bistorta-affinis/)); collected 5 km north-east of "Deosai lake" at 3,925 m, 17 July 1999 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4990638404)).
  - Reported uses: rhizome for diarrhoea and fever (Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).
  - `[general]` Creeping, densely tufted mats to about 25 cm tall; cylindrical spikes of pale pink to rose-red flowers from midsummer to autumn; Afghanistan to eastern Nepal at 3,000 to 4,800 m ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistorta_affinis)).

- **Violet monkshood** — *Aconitum violaceum* Jacquem. ex Stapf — Ranunculaceae — poisonous herb; blue-violet hooded flowers
  - Local names: **Buma** (Balti and Shina) ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)); **Booma** ([Bano 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4039317/)); **Bishmoulo** (Shina) ([Khan 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815990/)).
  - In the 1993 list of the plateau's vegetation cover `[paper]` ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf)); found in 5% of brown bear dropping samples ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - Eighteen specimen records from 1925 to 1999, e.g. 4,000 m beside the Chilam to Skardu track on 31 Aug 1995 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/5159762654)) and Stewart's sheet of 30 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4058677828)) `[specimen]`. Photographed at 4,200 m in July 2016 ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/12/aconitum-violaceum/)).
  - The 1998 wetlands expedition named a whole habitat after it: "Carex-Aconitum sedgeland" on wet peat at Sheosar Lake.
  - Listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List according to the UBC page. Reported uses: root for abdominal pain and flatulence (Balti); ringworm and typhoid (Shina); one of the five most-used species ([Abbas 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8153600/)).

- **Large-leaved primrose** — *Primula macrophylla* D.Don — Primulaceae — herb
  - Eleven specimen records, including the plateau at 4,000 m on 21 July 1998 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1096831253)) and Stewart's sheet of 30 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4851479398)) `[specimen]`.
  - Photographed on a mesic, north-east-facing upper slope at the northern edge at about 4,300 m on 28 July 2016 `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/13/primula-macrophylla/)).
  - Flower colour is not stated in the sources I could open.

- **Rosy primrose** — *Primula rosea* Royle — Primulaceae — herb of wet ground; pink flowers
  - Recorded from 3,770 to 4,170 m with IVI 4.99 (table row re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).

- **Catmints** — *Nepeta connata* Royle ex Benth. and *Nepeta discolor* Royle ex Benth. — Lamiaceae — aromatic herbs
  - *N. connata*: sixth most dominant species in 2016 (IVI 7.13, table row re-paired), an indicator of the lower dry sites, and it names the Taraxacum-Nepeta-Poa community `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)); on the GB Forest Department list.
  - *N. discolor*: QAU labels from Bara Pani ("Badapani"), 13 Aug 1985: "Herb, erect, flowers white, common" `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1913877137)).   - The UBC survey photographed a catmint at 4,200 m in an ungrazed area but could not settle the species ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/12/nepeta-laevigata/)).

- **Nodding dragonhead** — *Dracocephalum nutans* L. — Lamiaceae — herb
  - Collected on the plateau at 4,000 m on 21 July 1998 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1096674345)) and as high as 4,600 m on 15 July 1991 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615312766)) `[specimen]`.
  - On the GB Forest Department list (misspelled "Dracocephalum nutsus") ([GB Forest Dept](https://fwegb.gov.pk/deosai-national-park/)).

- **Lindelofias** — *Lindelofia longiflora* (Benth.) Baill. and *Lindelofia anchusoides* (Lindl.) Lehm. — Boraginaceae — herbs of the forget-me-not family
  - *L. longiflora* recorded from 3,790 to 4,170 m, IVI 3.94 (table row re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)); Stewart collected it at about 3,960 m on 28 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615351311)).
  - *L. anchusoides* photographed in the interior at about 4,000 m on 22 July 2016 `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/12/lindelofia-anchusoides/)).

- **Milk-vetches and locoweeds** — *Astragalus rhizanthus* Royle ex Benth., *Oxytropis tatarica* Cambess. ex Bunge, *Oxytropis cachemiriana* Cambess. — Fabaceae — low pea-flowered herbs
  - *A. rhizanthus* found in 5% and *O. cachemiriana* in 2% of brown bear dropping samples `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)).
  - *A. rhizanthus*: 14 specimen records, 1928 to 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4129413234)). *O. tatarica*: 12 records at 3,800 to 4,300 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615365349)) `[specimen]`.
  - A locoweed photographed at about 4,000 m on 23 July 2016 is labelled "probably" *O. cachemiriana* ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/12/oxytropis-cachemiriana/)).
  - *Astragalus melanostachys* is in the 1993 list of the plateau's vegetation ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf)) and in the 2016 survey (IVI 4.09).

- **Cinquefoils** — *Potentilla argyrophylla* Wall. ex Lehm. (including var. *atrosanguinea*) and *Potentilla anserina* L. — Rosaceae — herbs
  - Recorded with IVI 3.75 and 3.44 (table rows re-paired) `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - *P. argyrophylla* collected by Stewart on the Deosai Plains on 28 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4129382471)); another label reads "wet runoff area, Deosai Plateau between Skardu and Astore", 3,993 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/41413435)) `[specimen]`.
  - Local name of *P. argyrophylla* var. *atrosanguinea* in a Balti-language survey: **Serfo harswa**; whole plant used as a paste on wounds ([Bano 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4039317/)).

- **Long-stalked fumitories** — *Corydalis crithmifolia* Royle and other *Corydalis* — Papaveraceae — delicate herbs
  - *C. crithmifolia*: eight specimen records, e.g. the Skardu side of the plateau at 4,000 m on 21 July 1998 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1096790513)) and Stewart at about 3,960 m on 28 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4093687684)) `[specimen]`.
  - Seven further *Corydalis* names appear on plateau specimens (GBIF).

- **Dandelions** — *Taraxacum* spp., named *Taraxacum tibetanum* Hand.-Mazz. in Deosai papers — Asteraceae — rosette herbs; yellow heads
  - Third most dominant plant in 2016 (IVI 7.74) and it names the Taraxacum-Nepeta-Poa community at 3,781 m `[paper]` ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf)).
  - *T. tibetanum* was collected on the plateau in 2012 and 2013 ([Bano 2015](https://thejaps.org.pk/manuscript/2015-japs-60.pdf)). A "common dandelion" with yellow blossom appears in a UBC photo at about 4,000 m ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2021/07/17/pedicularis-pectinata-wall-ex-benth/)).
  - Dandelion species are hard to tell apart. "Dandelions (*Taraxacum*)" is the safe label.

- **River beauty and fireweed** — *Chamaenerion latifolium* (L.) Sweet (syn. *Epilobium latifolium*) and *Chamaenerion angustifolium* (L.) Scop. (syn. *Epilobium angustifolium*) — Onagraceae — willowherbs
  - Both listed from the Deosai plains in 1993 `[paper]` ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf)).
  - *C. latifolium* collected by Stewart on 30 July 1940 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/1322981295)) and on the plateau at 3,800 m on 13 Aug 1990 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615317509)) `[specimen]`.
  - *C. angustifolium* collected in wet sedgeland by the river below Bara Pani camp at 3,900 m and on a ridge at 4,200 m, Sept 1998 `[specimen]` (HO, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).

  - Nine specimen records at 3,850 to 4,300 m, e.g. Deosai Top Hotel at 3,930 m on 16 July 1999 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615319884)).

  - Found in 13% of brown bear dropping samples `[paper]` ([Nawaz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6932756/)); recorded at 3,780 m ([Shaheen 2019](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1538435113.pdf), which misplaces it in Chenopodiaceae).
  - Thirteen specimen records at 3,800 to 4,300 m, e.g. "Deosai Lake" at 4,100 m on 25 July 1994 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615293585)).
  - Photographed on an exposed, frost-churned upper slope at the northern edge at about 4,300 m on 28 July 2016, small white blossoms beside alpine fleabane `[photo]` ([UBC](https://blogs.ubc.ca/alpineplants/2020/05/12/cerastium-spp/)).

- **Iceland purslane** — *Koenigia islandica* L. — Polygonaceae — minute annual
  - Collected at Deosai at about 3,960 m by C.B. Clarke on 31 July 1876 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615342986)), and by R.R. Stewart on the Deosai Plains ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615342989)).

- **Alpine meadow-rue** — *Thalictrum alpinum* L. — Ranunculaceae — small wiry herb
  - Collected near Kala (Chala) Pani at 3,830 to 3,850 m on 16 to 17 July 1999 `[specimen]` ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615415077)).

## Part 4. Mosses, lichens, aquatic plants and fungi

### Mosses (Bryophyta)

All from herbarium specimens. The 1998 collections are by Terry A.J. Hedderson, made inside Deosai National Park.

- **Stewart's thread-moss** (no established English name) — *Pohlia stewartii* E.B.Bartram — Mniaceae. Collected by R.R. Stewart on the Deosai Plains at 13,000 ft on 29 July 1940; the species carries his name ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)). I did not confirm whether these sheets are the type.
- **Ringless hook-moss** — *Sarmentypnum exannulatum* (Schimp.) Hedenäs — Calliergonaceae. Five collections in Aug 1998: "floating in pools" and "floating in stagnant pools" in the mire-pool complexes at Sheosar Lake; "emergent from bog pool, abundant" in a sedge-dominated wetland about 5 km east of the junction of the Bara Pani and Kala Pani rivers (S, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).
- **Tundra hook-moss** (no established English name) — *Sarmentypnum tundrae* (Arnell) Hedenäs — Calliergonaceae. Four collections in Aug 1998: "emergent in shallow pools" in sedge-dominated mires on the west bank of the Bara Pani river just south of the road bridge; "forming mats along sluggish stream" in grazed sedge meadows at Bari La (S).
- **Kneiff's hook-moss and relatives** — *Drepanocladus aduncus* (Hedw.) Warnst., *D. polygamus* (Schimp.) Hedenäs, *D. capillifolius* (Warnst.) Warnst. — Amblystegiaceae. In shallow pools below Wolf Peak in the Bara Pani valley; "abundant along margins of shallow pools" by the Bara Pani river; on peaty sides of meltwater channels at Bari La, Aug 1998 (S).
- **Yellow starry feather-moss** — *Campylium stellatum* (Hedw.) Lange & C.E.O.Jensen — Amblystegiaceae. "Forming margin of deep pool", Bara Pani river valley, 5 Aug 1998 (S).
- **Brook-mosses of running water** — *Hygrohypnum duriusculum* (De Not.) D.W.Jamieson, "on rocks in swift stream, abundant" at Sheosar Lake; *Rhynchostegium muelleri* A.Jaeger, "on stones in riffles of outlet stream" at Sheosar Lake, 1 Aug 1998 (S).
- **Sickle-leaved hook-moss** — *Sanionia uncinata* (Hedw.) Loeske — Scorpidiaceae. Wet margins of a small stream below Wolf Peak, 17 Aug 1998 (S).
- **Juniper haircap and bristly haircap** — *Polytrichum juniperinum* Hedw. and *Polytrichum piliferum* Hedw. — Polytrichaceae. *P. juniperinum* collected by G. and S. Miehe in 1990 and 1991 in humid meadow with *Kobresia* and in "alpine Cyperaceae mats dominated by Carex and Kobresia" (H). *P. piliferum* collected along the Bara Pani river at 3,860 m and on a ridge at 4,290 m on 23 July 1998 (MO).
- **Rock mosses** — *Grimmia alpestris*, *G. elatior*, *G. longirostris*, *G. montana*, *G. ovalis*, *Schistidium confertum*, *Schistidium rivulare* — Grimmiaceae. All from a stream gully and ridge about 3 km east of the Bara Pani river at 3,925 to 4,290 m, 23 to 24 July 1998 (MO).
- **Screw-mosses of dry rock and soil** — *Syntrichia ruralis* (Hedw.) F.Weber & D.Mohr (a "rock moss on slate" on a summit at 3,945 m, 18 July 1999), *Syntrichia norvegica*, *Tortella fragilis*, *Didymodon asperifolius* (eastern Deosai plains, 15 July 1991, G. and S. Miehe) — Pottiaceae.
- **Thread-mosses** — *Pohlia cruda* (Hedw.) Lindb. and *Pohlia elongata* Hedw. — along the Bara Pani river at 3,800 m, 22 July 1998 (MO).
- **Bonfire moss** — *Funaria hygrometrica* Hedw. — Funariaceae. Named in the 1993 list of the plateau's vegetation cover, spelled "Funeria hygrometica" ([Farooq 1993](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2036%201993/Issue%2012/Article%209%20Vol%2036%20Issue%2012%201993%20pp%20535-536.pdf)). Single mention, no specimen found.

Shaheen et al. 2019 recorded "a single representative member" each of bryophytes, pteridophytes, mosses and lichens in their quadrats without naming them.

### Lichens (lichen-forming fungi)

- ***Oxneriaria deosaiensis*** Khalid & Usman — Megasporaceae. New species described in 2024 and named after Deosai. Irregular, cracked grey crusts 1.5 to 2 cm across; type collected on soil at 4,689 m on 13 May 2019, with a second collection on rock at 4,364 m ([Usman et al. 2024, MycoKeys 102: 285-299](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10924169/)).
- ***Oxneriaria crittendenii*** Usman & Khalid — Megasporaceae. Described in the same paper. Round thalli 1.5 to 5 cm across, light brown in the abstract's wording; on rocks at 4,177 to 4,651 m ([Usman et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10924169/)).
- ***Placidium deosaiense*** Usman & Khalid — Verrucariaceae. A soil-crust lichen described in 2021 from four localities in the park, with brown to blackish scales 2 to 7 mm wide ([Usman, Dyer & Khalid 2021, The Bryologist 124: 484-493](https://bioone.org/journals/the-bryologist/volume-124/issue-4/0007-2745-124.4.484/A-novel-arctic-alpine-lichen-from-Deosai-National-Park-Gilgit/10.1639/0007-2745-124.4.484.full)).
- ***Anamylopsora pakistanica*** Usman & Khalid — Baeomycetaceae. Described in 2023 from three localities in the park at 4,008 to 4,587 m, on calcareous rock; bright to dark brown scales 0.7 to 3 mm across. The authors report 4,587 m as the highest elevation known for the genus ([Usman et al. 2023, The Lichenologist](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/1FE4AAF0669D633FB6C426540D8F7797/core-reader)).
- **Earlier records listed in that 2023 paper:** *Acarospora anatolica* H.Magn., *Psora himalayana* (C.Bab.) Timdal, *Psora vallesiaca* (Schaer.) Timdal and *Pyrenodesmia micromontana* ([Usman et al. 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/1FE4AAF0669D633FB6C426540D8F7797/core-reader)).
- All four new species were described between 2021 and 2024, and the authors say only a small number of lichens had been documented from the Deosai Plains before.

### Aquatic and water-edge plants

All from herbarium specimens.

- **Red pondweed** — *Potamogeton alpinus* Balb. — Potamogetonaceae. Collected north-north-west of Kala (Chala) Pani camp at 3,850 m on 17 July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615377951)). An unnamed *Potamogeton* was collected in the upper Shatung valley at 4,200 m in Sept 1998 (HO).
- **A pondweed** — *Stuckenia amblyophylla* (C.A.Mey.) Holub — Potamogetonaceae. Same place and date, 3,850 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615378204)).
- **Mare's-tail** — *Hippuris vulgaris* L. — Plantaginaceae. Stewart's label of 29 July 1940 reads "A plant of slow moving water", Deosai Plains, about 3,960 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4851528520)); collected again at 3,850 m in July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615333709)).
- **Greater bladderwort** — *Utricularia vulgaris* L. — Lentibulariaceae — a carnivorous water plant. One record, 2 km north-north-west of Kala (Chala) Pani at 3,850 m, 17 July 1999 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/615420237)). Single record; bladderwort species are easily confused, so say "a bladderwort has been collected".
- **Vernal water-starwort** — *Callitriche palustris* L. — Plantaginaceae. In the "bed of seasonal peat pools along lake shore", east shore of Sheosar Lake, 4,050 m, 6 Sept 1998 ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2430753358)); upper Shatung valley, 4,200 m ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2430946946)).
- **Common water-crowfoot** — *Ranunculus aquatilis* L. — Ranunculaceae. "Muddy bottom of drying pools", west bank of the river below Bara Pani camp, 3,900 m, Sept 1998 (HO, via [GBIF search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search?q=Deosai&taxon_key=6)).
- **Mudwort** — *Limosella aquatica* L. — Scrophulariaceae. Same pools, same date, 3,900 m (HO).

### Fungi, mushrooms, algae

- I found no published species-level record of mushrooms, other non-lichen fungi, algae or diatoms from Deosai. The only fungi documented by name are the lichen-forming species above. Leave these groups out.

## Part 5. Buffer-zone and approach-valley species: do not present as growing on the plateau

These come from ethnobotanical surveys that interviewed people in villages around the park (Shila, Dapa, Sadpara, Sherkuli in Abbas et al. 2021; Das Khirim, Chilam, Chota Deosai and Sardar Kothi among the sites in Khan et al. 2018; nine Skardu-valley sites in Bano et al. 2014). The papers do not say which plants were gathered on the plateau. I found no plateau-specific evidence for the species below, or the evidence points to lower ground.

- **Trees and tall shrubs:** *Betula utilis* (Himalayan birch; Shina **Jongi**), *Juniperus excelsa* (Balti **Shukpa**, Shina **Chilli**), *Hippophae rhamnoides* (sea buckthorn; Balti **Karsokh**, Shina **Buru**), *Rosa webbiana* (Balti **Siya marfo**, Shina **Shighaye**), *Berberis pseudumbellata* and *B. orthobotrys* (Balti **Skiurbu**, Shina **Ishkeen**), *Ribes alpestre* (Balti **Askuta**, Shina **Churkani**). The only *Betula utilis* specimens with "Deosai" on the label are from "Deosai Nullah, Skardu" at 3,658 m in 1978, the stream valley on the Skardu approach ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/2859399736)). Farooq 1993 places *Rosa webbiana* "Skardu to Deosai plains".
- **Artemisia brevifolia** (Balti **Bursay**, Shina **Zoon**): Farooq 1993 gives Satpara Dam; the specimen is from the upper Shingo valley above Gulteri ([GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/4601974384)).
- **Village and field plants:** *Fagopyrum esculentum* (buckwheat; Balti **Bro**), *Mentha royleana* (Balti **Foling**, Shina **Phileel**), *Solanum nigrum*, *Urtica dioica*, *Trifolium repens*, *Taraxacum officinale*, *Rubia cordifolia*, *Carum carvi* and *Carum bulbocastanum* (Balti **Naqpo thalay** and **Karpho thalay**, Shina **Hayyo**), *Cichorium intybus*, *Plantago lanceolata*, *Capparis spinosa*, *Perovskia abrotanoides*, *Sophora mollis*, *Lepidium latifolium*.
- **Lower-valley medicinal plants in Khan et al. 2018:** *Ferula narthex*, *Ferula jaeschkeana*, *Colutea paulsonii*, *Fragaria nubicola*, *Mentha longifolia*, *Equisetum arvense*, *Hyoscyamus niger*, *Colchicum luteum*, *Angelica glauca*, *Chaerophyllum villosum*, *Astragalus bicuspis*, *Astragalus peduncularis*, *Juniperus communis*.
- **Sinopodophyllum hexandrum** (Himalayan mayapple, syn. *Podophyllum emodi*): Farooq 1993 lists "Kalapani, Deosai, Burzil top"; the specimens say "Deosai Plains, Chilum" and an 1838 "Deosir". Chilam is the Astore-side approach.
- **Colchicum, kuth and other Astore-side herbs** in Farooq 1993 (Gudai, Rattu, Chilam, Burzil): not plateau records.

Species from these surveys that DO have independent plateau evidence are in Parts 2 and 3, with their local names.

## Part 6. Unconfirmed or doubtful: leave out, or use only with the stated caveat

### Reported by a peer-reviewed Deosai paper, but doubtful

These appear in Table 1 of Shaheen et al. 2019 at 3,770 to 4,200 m. Each is a lowland, warm-climate, coastal, garden or otherwise out-of-range plant, and none is supported by a plateau specimen I could find. They are most likely misidentifications. Leave out.

- *Capparis himalayensis* (a hot dry-valley shrub; listed at 4,170 m)
- *Thelepogon elegans* (a tropical grass)
- *Cuscuta reflexa* (a lowland dodder; the paper calls it the only "epiphyte")
- *Fumaria indica*, *Oxalis corniculata*, *Borago officinalis*, *Arctium lappa*, *Artemisia annua*, *Medicago falcata*, *Polygonum aviculare*, *Thlaspi perfoliatum* (lowland weeds or garden plants)
- *Juncus maritimus* (a coastal rush)
- *Rosa brunonii* (a climbing rose of lower forests)
- *Rubus saxatilis*, *Anemone pulsatilla*, *Leontopodium alpinum* (European names; the last is the European edelweiss)
- *Polygonatum verticillatum* (a forest plant; the one specimen with a Deosai header is from 3,353 m)
- "*Polygonum aquileum*", "*Anaphalis arvensis*", "*Dactylorhiza glabra*", "*Salix himalensis*" (names I could not match to any accepted species)

### Reported by one source only; plausible but not confirmed

- *Saussurea costus* (kuth, syn. *S. lappa*): listed at about 3,780 to 3,880 m and named as IUCN-threatened by Shaheen et al. 2019. Kuth is a plant of lower subalpine slopes and Farooq 1993 gives only Astore-side localities. Wikipedia lists it for the park with no citation. Do not present it as a plateau plant without the caveat "reported in one 2016 survey".
- *Lilium polyphyllum*: listed by Shaheen et al. 2019 at about 3,880 to 3,990 m and named as IUCN-threatened. It is a forest-zone lily. No specimen found. Leave out.
- *Dactylorhiza hatagirea* (Himalayan marsh orchid; Shina **Narmada** in Khan 2018): listed at 4,150 m by Shaheen et al. 2019. Plausible in wet meadows, but I found no specimen or photo from the plateau. Use only as "reported in a 2016 survey".
- *Iris hookeriana* (Shina **Gusman** in Khan 2018): listed at 3,770 to 3,800 m by Shaheen et al. 2019. No specimen found. Same caveat.
- *Aconitum chasmanthum*: listed by Shaheen et al. 2019 (IVI 1.75) and supported by one 1999 database record at 3,850 m. Reasonable, but the UBC notes show how often Deosai monkshoods are re-identified.
- *Arnebia benthamii* (Balti **Thang marsi**, Shina **Kazaban**): two 1928 Osmaston records, one at 4,115 m. Old records only.
- *Saussurea obvallata* (Brahma kamal): in the 2015 leaf study of the plateau "and its adjacent areas"; the only specimen is from "Bari La near Deosai Plains" (Duthie, undated). The UBC photos of it are from Ghondghoro, not Deosai.
- *Pleurospermum candollei* (Balti **Shamdun**): listed at 3,990 m by Shaheen et al. 2019; the UBC photo caption says "Deosai valley, 4300 m" and the author doubts the identification. Specimens from the plateau are filed as *Pleurospermum stellatum* instead.
- *Swertia perfoliata*: named for valley bogs by Fida et al. 2025. Every specimen and photo is *Swertia petiolata*; this is probably the same plant under a slip of the pen.
- *Corydalis govaniana*: UBC caption reads "Desai Plateau", 4,300 m. Almost certainly a typing slip for Deosai, but the post does not literally say so.
- *Tanacetum falconeri* (Balti **Tyalo**, Shina **Flagyl** in Abbas 2021): the UBC post is from Thalle valley, not Deosai. No plateau evidence.
- *Bupleurum longicaule*, *Polygonum paronychioides*, *Ranunculus adoxifolius*, *Silene moorcroftiana*, *Lactuca lessertiana* (now *Melanoseris lessertiana*): each has a UBC Deosai photo and most have specimens. They are probably fine, but I have not written full entries. Usable as names with the UBC links in the source list.

### On official or popular lists but unverified

The Gilgit-Baltistan Forest Department page and Wikipedia carry the same list of about 21 names, with no source given. Status of each:

- **Confirmed elsewhere in this file:** *Polygonum affine*, *Thalictrum alpinum*, *Saxifraga flagellaris*, *Androsace mucronifolia*, *Aster flaccidus*, *Nepeta connata*, *Dracocephalum nutans*, *Agropyron longearistatum* / *Elymus longiaristatus*, *Artemisia maritima*.
- **Supported only by weak or off-plateau records:** *Carex cruenta* (two 1999 records near Kala/Chala Pani at 3,800 to 3,830 m; reasonable), *Arenaria neelgherrensis* (a 1978 QAU sheet from Deosai top; the name belongs to a South Indian plant, so the identification is suspect), *Bromus oxyodon* (the only record is from the Shingo valley at 3,050 m), *Barbarea vulgaris* (one undated database record; QAU sheets from Bara Pani are *Barbarea intermedia*), *Senecio chrysanthemoides* (collected for the 2015 leaf study; no specimen found), *Polygonum amplexicaule* (written "amplexinade").
- **No evidence found:** *Ranunculus laetus*, *Astragalus leucocephalus*, *Echinops niveus*, *Chrysopogon gryllus* subsp. *echinulatus*, *Dianthus crinitus*, *Anaphalis contorta*.
- **Error:** Wikipedia's "*Taxus contorta*" (a yew tree). The Forest Department page it mirrors reads "Anapalas contorta", i.e. *Anaphalis contorta*, a pearly everlasting. There is no yew on the plateau.

### Other claims to avoid

- "Hundreds of species of medicinal and aromatic plants" (UNESCO, GB Forest Department): the three field surveys found 47 to 51 medicinal species. Quote it only as UNESCO's wording.
- Any statement about pollinators. None of the sources I read documents pollinators of Deosai plants. UNESCO mentions rare butterflies in the same sentence as the wildflowers but does not link the two.
- Any named mushroom, alga or diatom.
- *Conyza japonica*, *Cirsium falconeri* and *Artemisia persica* (2015 leaf study): collected from the plateau "and its adjacent areas"; no plateau specimen found for the first two. *Artemisia persica* has a 1946 Stewart record at 3,353 m.

The larger figures are for the national park as now demarcated; the plateau and the park are not the same thing. |
