    II  —  The house

    A whole opera house, built at one-twelfth the size. The stage is as wide as an open newspaper. The singers standing behind it are the usual size.

    Quire began in 2017 with a secondhand toy theatre and an argument about whether Tosca needed a chorus. It turned out it needed forty people, which was the end of the argument, and the beginning of this.

    We work in a former bindery on Falzgasse. The stage sits on the old guillotine table. Four singers and six players stand behind a gauze, unamplified, close enough that you will hear them breathe in before the high notes.

    A quire is twenty-four sheets of paper. It is also an old spelling of choir. We did not plan the second part.

    Rusalka, act one  ·  exploded

    Seven sheets deep

    Every scene is seven layers of paper standing in grooves, thirty millimetres apart. Nothing is painted to look far away. It simply is further away.

      III  —  The workshop

      From sheet
      to scene

      One production takes about eleven hundred hours and forty sheets. This is the Dutchman's ship, four times, on the same table.

      01/ 04

      1. 01

        Draw

        Each scene starts as a single pencil drawing at full size, which is to say very small. If it doesn't read from the back row — four metres — it is drawn again.

        140 hours of drawing per opera

      2. 02

        Cut

        Everything is cut with a scalpel on bare cartridge paper first, to find out where it will tear. A blade lasts about forty minutes. We keep the dull ones in a jar, for humility.

        300 blades, give or take

      3. 03

        Colour

        Paper first, paint second. Sugar paper for skies, mulberry for mist, and gouache only where the mill doesn't make the colour. Each opera is allowed five colours and one accent.

        5 + 1 colours, no exceptions

      4. 04

        Light

        Thirty-eight lamps, each the size of a lentil. Once they are on, the edges of the paper start to glow and the scissors stop mattering. That is the moment we are working towards.

        38 lamps, one dimmer, one hand

      “People ask whether it is fiddly. It is exactly as fiddly as an orchestra, only smaller and it doesn't need lunch.”

      Henrike Vossberg, who cuts every sheet
